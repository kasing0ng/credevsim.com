Can you even use the loss?

A syndication is passive. With no passive income, §469 suspends that year-one loss — unless you or your spouse qualify for Real Estate Professional Status (REPS) with material participation. For a limited-partnership interest, that is the only door.

Your real after-tax number

Net of recapture, NIIT, and state. On a cost-seg-heavy deal that's routinely points of IRR below the pitch.

The exit-year tax spike

Early depreciation comes back as recapture at sale. The taxable gain can dwarf the economic gain. Forecast it early.

Why this seat is empty

The sponsor is selling, which makes the deck a sales document, not a lie. Their CPA files the partnership return and cuts the K-1s. Your CPA files whatever lands on your K-1. Nobody in that chain is paid to read the deal from the limited partner's tax seat before you wire, on your bracket, with your other income. That empty seat is the whole job. The sponsor is conflicted, not crooked; I'm simply independent of the raise.

The loss can be blocked three times

Before a dollar of that year-one loss reaches the passive-activity rules, it has to clear two earlier gates: your outside basis under §704(d), then your at-risk amount under §465. Only what survives both is tested under §469. An LP can be told the deal “throws off a big loss” and still deduct none of it this year. I check all three, in order, against your actual basis and at-risk amount, not the number on the deck.

Federal is only half the bill

Everything the deck shows is the federal read. Your state can be a different answer entirely. California, New York, and New Jersey do not conform to federal bonus depreciation, so the year-one loss on your federal K-1 can be smaller or absent on the state return, and a loss suspended on federal Form 8582 can be suspended differently for state. The §1250 25% figure is a federal rate; states tax recapture and capital gain their own way, often as ordinary income with no special rate. I model the state layer too — your state of residence and the deal's state — not just the federal number.

A deal winding down, foreclosing, or being sold triggers the tax you've been deferring all along, and the suspended losses you've been piling up. The timing between them decides whether you owe or walk clean.

Wind-down & distress

Exit-Year & Suspended-Loss Workout

quoted, by situation

Those suspended passive losses are an asset you forgot you own. When you fully dispose of the activity, the whole suspended amount generally releases, and it has to line up against the gain, the depreciation recapture, and any cancellation-of-debt income in the same year. Get the timing and the basis right and a painful-looking exit can net to little or nothing. Get it wrong and you pay tax on money you never saw. I pull your K-1s and basis history and map the release.

  • The suspended §469 losses freed at full disposition, matched against the exit-year gain
  • Recapture and cancellation-of-debt income on a foreclosure or deed-in-lieu, recourse versus nonrecourse
  • Negative capital account and the gain it can force even when you receive nothing
  • Insolvency and timing levers, mapped for you to take to your preparer
