Common questions

Before you book.

Do you file my taxes?

No. This is advisory and non-attest work: no returns, no audits, no reviews. I model the after-tax outcome at underwriting and work alongside the CPA who files, not instead of them.

I already have a CPA. Why do I need this?

Your CPA files after the year closes and looks backward at what already happened. I model forward, at underwriting, before you sign, so the tax outcome informs the go/no-go instead of surprising you at K-1 time. I work alongside your filing CPA, not instead of them.

How is this different from a cost-seg firm?

A cost-seg firm sells you the study, so the answer is always yes. I have nothing to sell you at exit. The read stays independent of whether a study actually pays for itself on your deal.

What do you need from me?

The deck or offering memo, the rent roll or T-12, and the debt terms. From that I model the pre-tax and after-tax LP return, the recapture at exit, and the go/no-go on cost seg.

What does a Deal Tax Projection cost?

A flat $750 at the current founder rate: one price, no hourly meter, and no markup on a cost-seg study. You get the pre-tax and after-tax LP return, the recapture at exit, and a clear go/no-go before you commit capital.

How long does it take?

A few business days from the time I have the deck, rent roll and debt terms. If you're up against a signing deadline, tell me and I'll work to it.

Do you work with GPs or LPs?

Both. Mostly sponsors and GPs pressure-testing a deal they're about to raise on, but also LPs who want an independent read on a sponsor's projected returns before they wire.

Is my deal data safe?

Yes, two ways. If I run the projection for you, your deck, rent roll and debt terms are confidential client material: I never share them with third parties, and I'm glad to sign an NDA before you send anything. If you'd rather keep the file in-house, the engine itself runs 100% local on a Windows desktop, so the deal file never leaves your machine and never touches a server.

Are you a licensed CPA?

Yes: an active California CPA (license 139270) and a former Big 4 real estate auditor. This is advisory, non-attest work, so what I hand you is analysis to use alongside your filing CPA and counsel, not an audit or a tax return.

I already own the property. Can you still help?

Yes. On an aged hold I check the depreciation a Form 3115 §481(a) catch-up can still recover in a single year, plus the recapture math on a sale or 1031 exit.
